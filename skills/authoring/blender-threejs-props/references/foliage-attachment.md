# Foliage and believable attachment

Randomness needs an underlying biological or construction structure.

For palms, establish trunk taper and overlapping leaf bases, dense upright central growth, rising middle fronds and a few older drooping lower fronds. Vary frond paths, curvature changes, length, twist and tip damage. Offset leaflet starts, spacing and angles asymmetrically; allow gaps and overlapping leaflets. Perfect parabolas and mirrored evenly spaced leaflets look manufactured.

Use correlated variation at plant/frond/leaflet scales rather than independent white noise everywhere. Preserve named crown/frond identities in lower detail levels. Reduce small leaflets or card complexity while retaining the major crown silhouette and density. Check transparent/cutout overdraw and shadow cost in the performance workflow.

Place weeds in protected joints, cracks, planting areas and plausible water/light conditions. Avoid regular rows unless cultivated. Ground the base and roots using the actual sloping surface; vary clumps within a coherent species rather than random rods.

For all assets, visible construction needs attachment: cloth ties meet anchors, rails meet posts, cables reach insulators, signs have brackets, benches have feet. Inspect these at the intended view. Rotating or moving an assembly must move its supports and paint coordinates with it.

For leaning/stacked props, compose the authored lean with placement rotation and receiving slope, then check actual support points. Do not assume the bounding-box minimum is the contact patch; use the helper's transformed facts and a visual inspection together.

## Match the foliage technique to the art direction

A small polygon budget does not imply a faceted low-poly appearance. Establish whether the brief wants solid foliage masses, textured cards, or camera-facing billboards before refining the crown. Judge texture mark size on the assembled tree at the game camera: repeating a detailed branch sprite many times can turn a simple painted source into noisy tiny tufts. Fewer larger groups may preserve the intended abstraction better.

Distinguish card orientation from billboard terminology. For upward-facing pine boughs, align the leaf cards with the branches and inspect top, side, and end profiles; do not substitute upright camera-facing crossed planes. When the brief calls for a gabled crease, put the ridge along the twig and let the card sides curve downward into flatter outer edges. Keep the painted strokes radiating outward from that twig. This is a reference-specific construction option, not a rule for every species.

Inspect the generated file's actual channels. A painted checkerboard in an RGB image is not transparency. Obtain genuine alpha or author an explicit cutout mask, then verify the mapped foliage against light and dark backgrounds. Check edge halos, shadows and deep alpha overlap in both the final render and the target viewport. Increasing offline transparent-ray depth alone does not establish efficient runtime foliage.

For a card-based pine, do not duplicate a textured twig with conspicuous solid branch geometry unless its silhouette needs that support. Separate three checks: the surface normal can face upward while the radial branch tip droops downward; transverse gabled curvature is different from longitudinal droop; and crown density depends on overlapping whorls, not just a billboard count. Verify both profiles and the full tree. If the brief asks lighting to supply color variation, keep leaf base color consistent instead of baking bright and dark streaks into every repeated sprite.

Check upper-crown density separately from lower-crown density. Uniformly shrinking successive branch cards can leave an exposed, antenna-like leader: compensate with more overlapping, irregularly offset upper attachments. Smaller young branches may also need a narrower fan and more delicate twig or stroke width; uniform scaling alone does not express thinner growth. Preserve the approved lower silhouette while reviewing the upper crown from a horizontal view and the park camera.

For taller tree variants, measure crown width and height together. Increasing only vertical scale can turn tiered branches into a narrow spiral or expose excessive gaps; clearance is not a reason to accept a distorted species silhouette. Establish a plausible height-to-crown-width range from the approved tree and art direction, inspect an isolated equal-scale comparison, then move placement anchors or reduce overall size when a fuller crown conflicts with neighboring props. Record any intentional nonuniform scaling and judge its visible result.
