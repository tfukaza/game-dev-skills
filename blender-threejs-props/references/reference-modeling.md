# Reference isolation and modular modeling

For a sheet or scene containing many props, identify the single target before modeling. Use the existing image-generation workflow to isolate it against a simple background when that will clarify silhouette or construction. Preserve the target's proportions, material families and identifying features; do not accidentally generate a redesign.

Build a canonical view and enough alternate views to resolve occluded structure. Specify consistent size, feature count, construction, artwork placement and camera intent. Generated rear/side views are hypotheses. Resolve contradictions against the authoritative source and a simple 3D blockout; do not average incompatible designs or claim generated dimensions are measurements.

Split a complex building into assemblies such as doorway, arch/window bay, railing, coping, fixture and roof component. Give components named local axes, pivots, nominal dimensions and attachment anchors. Assemble from authored transforms; do not fit each finished model into an arbitrary bounding box.

Model broad shape, bevels, recesses, panel thickness, large folds and silhouette damage. Put grain, weave and shallow relief in materials. Review a neutral/clay version at intended viewing distances, then finish one prototype through export before multiplying the kit.

Keep isolated references, generation prompts, canonical artwork, material graphs and editable meshes associated with the source workshop. These are authoring inputs, not runtime dependencies unless explicitly used.
